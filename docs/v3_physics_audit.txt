1. FASTENERS - thread engagement, measured from the holes that were actually printed
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
   Thread-forming (self-tapping) screws into printed PLA want a pilot at the thread's MINOR
   diameter, not at nominal: at nominal the crest only just touches the wall, the joint is friction,
   and the screw spins instead of biting.  Pull-out = shear of the plastic around the engaged thread
   cylinder, with PLA cross-layer shear 8.5 MPa and 55 % thread fill:
   F = tau * pi * d_minor * L_engagement * 0.55

4. AIRFLOW - one opening on purpose, and what cooling costs without the grilles
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
   v3.3 cut 8 + 4 slots so the fan could push air through the box.  v3.4 fills every one of them in:
   the user has a 3010 blower and wants one hole, not twelve.  So the question this section has to
   answer is not "is the grille big enough" but "does the box still shed its heat with a single
   opening", and it is answered with two paths computed from the meshes:
   PASSIVE - heat conducts through the PLA skin and leaves the box by free convection plus
   radiation, dT = P / (h A_skin).  This path does not care about openings at all, which is exactly
   why deleting twelve of them is not the disaster it looks like.
   THROUGH-FLOW - the fan still moves air, in through the bore and out through the one gap a
   printed box cannot avoid: the register seam around the rear plate.  Modelled as sharp-edged
   orifices in series with the fan's straight line, dp = dp_max (1 - Q/Q_max) and
   dp = K rho/2 (Q/A_eff)^2, K = 1.3.  The seam area is ASSUMED (see its own row), everything else
   here is measured.
   The two paths are in parallel, so their conductances add.
   the +X wall is still plain                 0 void(s) in the right-hand wall at mid-thickness      PASS
   the grilles really are filled in           0 slot(s) in the bottom wall and 0 in the top wall at mid-thickness; v3.3 measured 8 and 4, v3.4 asked for none PASS
   the USB opening survived the change        1 void(s) of cable opening, largest 20.40 x 12.40 mm against a designed 20.4 x 12.4 (18.0 x 10.0 slot plus 1.2 mm per side) PASS
   the fan bore is the one deliberate air opening a d28.00 circle of 615 mm2 in the -X wall, and it is the only air hole on the box PASS
   inventory off the mesh - bottom wall: 20.4 x 12.4 mm (247 mm2)   top wall: none
                     +X wall: none   -X wall: 28.0 x 28.0 mm (615 mm2)
   direction matters at build time: the fan has to blow IN through that bore, so the arrows on its frame point at the box.  The enclosure then runs a little above ambient pressure, which is what keeps dust out of the R307's prism and forces the used air back out through the plate seam.
   the bore is now the biggest hole on the box, and it is closed by the fan itself: a 30 x 30 frame over a d28 opening spans it completely.  That makes the fan a structural part of the enclosure rather than an accessory - a shell printed and used without it has a 28 mm hole in the side.
   the skin alone gets the heat out           1.6 W over 0.0585 m2 of outer wall at h = 10 W/m2.K -> dT = 2.7 K above ambient.  The 12 K ceiling is where an ESP32 crystal starts to drift and a humid Mirzapur morning fogs the R307 prism; at h = 5 W/m2.K (a closed cabinet, no air movement at all) it is 5.5 K, still inside it.  Wall plastic is the resistor here, not the openings, which is why filling twelve of them in costs so little PASS
   both paths together                        skin 2.7 K and flow 5.4 K in parallel -> dT = 1.8 K at 1.6 W; v3.3 measured 1.6 K through the grilles, so closing them costs about 0.2 K PASS
   ASSUMED row, labelled as one: the outlet is the plate seam, 509 mm of register frame with 0.25 mm of clearance per side = 127 mm2.  Your print decides that number - a 0.10 mm tighter fit takes 40 % of the leak away - so it is recorded as a note, never as a gate.  What is measured is the bore, and the absence of everything else.
   0.25 L/s through that seam changes the box's whole 784 cm3 volume every 3.2 s, i.e. 25 % of the fan's free air at 12.2 Pa.  v3.3 got 0.84 L/s out of 533 mm2 of effective area; the 615 mm2 bore was already the limit, which is the whole reason this trade was affordable.
   reading of these two rows, not a gate: through-flow is no longer what keeps this box cool.  What the fan does inside a sealed box is break the boundary layer off the LCD and the R307 prism and hold the enclosure slightly above room pressure, and with it switched off the skin path alone still sits 9.3 K inside the ceiling - so 0.1 W of fan is still worth spending for the demisting, which is why the print order runs it continuously from the ESP32's own supply
   nothing can be reached through the air path either: the 615 mm2 bore is spanned by the fan frame once built, and the only other hole is the USB slot, which is 12.4 mm tall and sits behind a plug.

5. MATERIAL LIMITS AND THE PRINTED FIT
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
   This unit is going on a wall in Uttar Pradesh, so the ambient numbers below matter more than the
   CAD numbers: PLA's useful ceiling is its glass transition (~60 C), and its 0.2-0.5 % shrinkage is
   what the printed fits have to absorb.
   PLA margin - shaded indoor wall, peak summer Tg ~60 C vs 45 C = +15 K                               PASS
   PLA margin - sun through a window onto the wall Tg ~60 C vs 55 C = +5 K                                PASS
   PLA margin - unshaded outside wall / closed room Tg ~60 C vs 65 C = -5 K   -> print PETG (Tg ~80 C) or anneal at 80 C for 1 h NOTE
   no geometry change is needed to switch to PETG: same model, 230-240 C nozzle, 80-90 C
   bed, and the print order's temperatures are the only thing that changes.
   differential thermal growth across the register frame both parts are the same plastic so the 0.37 mm of growth on a 155 mm side is common-mode and cancels; only a temperature DIFFERENCE bites (5 K -> 0.031 mm vs 0.25 mm per side) PASS
   shrinkage 0.2 % (dry filament, tuned) vs the plate's register 0.21 mm on the 104.8 mm opening; the frame is 8 separate ribs and the plate is drawn home by 4 x M3, so an oversized rib cannot lock the plate out PASS
   shrinkage 0.5 % (humid filament, Mirzapur monsoon) vs the plate's register 0.52 mm on the 104.8 mm opening; the frame is 8 separate ribs and the plate is drawn home by 4 x M3, so an oversized rib cannot lock the plate out PASS
   wall thickness vs shrinkage: a 2.6 mm wall varies by 0.005-0.013 mm - irrelevant.  The
   fit that must survive shrinkage is the 0.25 mm/side register and the 0.35 mm part fit,
   both of which are generous for FDM and both still verified in section 10 of the STL audit.

6. RF - 13.56 MHz through the wall, 2.4 GHz out of the cavity, metal nearby
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
   Dielectric loss in a slab: alpha = (2 pi f / c) sqrt(eps_r) tan(delta) / 2, loss = 8.686 alpha t.
   PLA at these frequencies is nearly invisible; what matters is geometry - how much plastic is in
   front of the coil, and how far the nearest metal is from the loop.
   13.56 MHz (RC522) through 2.20 mm of PLA   0.0001 dB (eps_r 2.6, tan d 0.02)                      PASS
   2.44 GHz (Wi-Fi) through 3.00 mm of PLA    0.0214 dB (eps_r 2.6, tan d 0.02)                      PASS
   nearest steel screw to the antenna loop    heads are 38.0 mm from the coil centre vs a ~25 mm loop -> 25.5 mm clear; keep the ring screws at 8 mm so no shank runs beside the coil plane PASS
   ESP32_RF_ANTENNA_KEEP_OUT empty of printed material 0 of 4350 points inside the 15 mm slab in front of the trace (y -18.5..-3.5, the whole cavity depth in x) - nothing printed stands in it, and the two antenna-end bosses sit BEHIND the board, beside it PASS
   metal at the antenna end                   the two antenna-end screw heads are 10.7 mm from the trace end, so M2.2 x 8 mm screws only - a long shank running beside the trace pulls the 2.4 GHz match; no brass or steel washers under those two heads PASS
   the reader's stated working distance (up to 10 mm for a card, ~2.5 mm for a tag) is
   reduced by the 2.2 mm ledge only in the front half of the board; the aperture
   puts AIR in front of the coil's centre, which is why the opening was made 100 % clear.

7. WHAT THE USER AND THE SENSORS CAN SEE
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
   Fingerprint: the R307 is an optical (reflective prism) module, so it needs an unobstructed cone
   from its window to where a finger lands, and nothing in front of the glass to scatter light.
   LCD: a stock 1602 has a narrow contrast cone; the window must not clip the character area even
   when the box is looked up at from below, because it is wall-mounted at about eye height.
   R307: 20 deg cone to 8 mm proud of the face is clear 0 of 1815 samples inside material; the 21.0 x 25.0 mm bezel relief is what keeps the 19.3 x 21.2 mm window unobstructed PASS
   LCD: the whole 64 x 14 character area is readable 2275/2275 (point, angle) samples pass; the window stops the view only beyond +/-34 deg horizontally and +/-49 deg vertically (measured at the wall's mid-plane), and the panel's own contrast cone is narrower than that PASS
   the window is 1.03x the character width and 1.25x their height, and the
   0.45 x 3.0 mm rebate sinks the glass 0.45 mm
   below the surface, so glare is reduced and the module is protected by a real frame - the
   two things v2 got wrong at the same time (its aperture was blocked by bars AND the glass
   was flush and chippable).

   these boxes actually crack).
