Section 1

Problem focus

What exactly is broken, and how would we know it got better?
{{i:pen-line}} Suggested answers — replace with your measured values

Manual attendance can be time-consuming and vulnerable to recording errors or proxy attendance. The team proposes an AI-supported system to record and manage attendance.

Discovery question

Who experiences this problem, when does it occur,
what do they currently do, and what evidence
would show that the problem has improved?

{{i:users}} Who

Who experiences the problem? Name the roles, not “everyone”.

Class teachers (main); also the school office / attendance clerk who compiles registers and the principal who signs them.
{{i:clock}} When

When does it occur — which period, day, season, or event?

Every school day, in the first-period / morning registration window; worst when a teacher is busy, covering another class, or absent and a substitute takes over.
{{i:book-open}} What they do now

What is the current workaround? Paper register, roll call, group leaders?

Paper register + loud roll-call; group leaders mark their friends; entries are copied later or erased by hand.
{{i:target}} Evidence of improvement

What number or observation would prove the problem got smaller?

Roll-call time falls from 10–15 min to under 2 min per class; no missing or late entries over 5 school days; hand corrections drop to 0; all 30 students accounted for without a manual headcount.
{{i:lightbulb}} Our problem statement

Write one sentence a stranger could repeat back to you. If it has a number in it, even better.

“Every class spends 10–15 minutes a day on paper roll-call, and because anyone can mark the register, we cannot trust that it shows who was really present.”

Section 5

Selected concept and reason

One concept, named and defended — with the privacy line drawn in advance.
Selected concept

AttendSure · Privacy-First Attendance

A privacy-first attendance system using a rotating QR code displayed by the teacher. Students scan during a short attendance window; the system records time, class and a pseudonymous student ID. The teacher reviews exceptions and confirms the register. Offline mode stores entries locally and syncs later. Avoid facial recognition in the student prototype.

{{i:qr-code}}

Rotating QR code

A fresh classroom code, so yesterday’s screenshot is useless today.

{{i:timer}}

Short window

Scans count only inside the attendance period set by the teacher.

{{i:key-round}}

Pseudonymous ID

A stable student token — not a name, photo or biometric — is what gets recorded.

{{i:clipboard-check}}

Teacher confirms

Exceptions surface to the teacher, who approves the final register.

{{i:wifi-off}}

Offline mode

Entries are stored on the device and synced when the network returns.

{{i:eye-off}}

No face recognition

Deliberately excluded from the student prototype — privacy and consent come first.

{{i:flag}} Selection rationale
“It addresses the registered problem, can be represented by a student-scale prototype, and offers measurable outcomes.”

Confirm this choice with evidence from the team’s discovery dialogues before building anything.

Team reflection

What changed our minds

Answer these together, in sentences — not bullets.
{{i:x-circle}} 1 · Which idea did we reject, and why?
Face recognition — storing children’s biometrics raises privacy and consent concerns we cannot safeguard in a student prototype; RFID / NFC would need a device every student must carry.
{{i:message-circle}} 2 · What did a user tell you that changed your thinking?
A teacher told us she skips roll-call when she is busy and fills it in later — so the real problem is time under pressure, not the paper itself.
{{i:target}} 3 · What is the smallest test that can prove or disprove our main assumption?
Run one class of 30 students through the QR flow and time it; if the teacher cannot finish in under 2 minutes and confirm the exceptions easily, our main assumption is wrong.
